# 0038: Organizations add their own domains, verified by a provisioner the installation supplies

Status: accepted (2026-10-11), partly implemented (see Consequences)

## Context

An organization's learners practise on its learn domains, `fernwood.braivo.app` given at setup and, on its own, `learn.fernwood.example` ([ADR 0004](0004-one-application-origin.md)). Until now the operator registered each one by command (`braivo organization add-domain`), having checked who asked, and a registered domain served at once. On Braivo Cloud, schools are to add their own from the console's settings, without asking anyone, and Cloud serves them through Cloudflare for SaaS: Cloudflare issues each hostname's certificate once its DNS points at Cloud's fallback origin. Braivo is open source and must run without Cloudflare, so the core cannot call it. The maintainer decided the points below (2026-10-11), after review.

## Decision

- **An organization's `owner` or `admin` adds and removes its domains** in the console, at `/<organization>/settings/domains`, which lists those serving it, the primary one first: the latest registered, which new links to learners, invitations included, name (a sign-in stays on the domain it began on, ADR 0004).
- **The installation verifies, through a provisioner it passes to `createServer`**, as it passes `sendMail`: adding a hostname, reading its status and the DNS records the organization must create, and removing it. Braivo Cloud implements it with Cloudflare's custom hostnames API, its records pointing at `domains.braivo.app`; another installation may implement it with whatever serves its hosts. An installation without one lists domains read-only, and the operator's command still registers them.
- **An added domain is a claim, kept apart until proven**, in `organization_domain_claim` (its hostname, organization, and when claimed; its status is the provisioner's, read live, never copied). `organization_domain` keeps meaning "serves": no reader of it, trusted origins and learner sessions among them, needs a status filter it could forget, and a claim serves nothing, is trusted for nothing, and names no learn domain.
- **A hostname is one organization's, across both tables.** Claiming, activating, and the operator's command each take a transaction-scoped lock on the hostname, then check both tables: a hostname claimed or served by another organization is refused.
- **A claim not activated within seven days expires**, so claiming alone never holds a hostname: expired, it reserves nothing and cannot be activated, whatever the provisioner says of it. The hostname's next claim replaces it, under the hostname's lock, so nothing runs on a schedule; what the provisioner holds for an expired claim is its own to sweep. The operator may cancel a pending claim, having checked who asks, never a domain serving.
- **Every claim proves ownership afresh**, by a record the provisioner names for it, such as a TXT token, before it can be activated: DNS already pointing at the installation, or a hostname the provisioner already serves, does not suffice, since a CNAME left behind by an organization that has gone would otherwise hand its hostname to whoever claims it next. Ready, as the provisioner reports it, means proven, its traffic reaching the installation, and its certificate issued.
- **Activating is an explicit write**, `POST`, which asks the provisioner and, once ready, inserts the domain and deletes its claim in one transaction, idempotently; reading a claim's status changes nothing. Inserted then, it becomes the primary domain only then: new links to learners never name a domain that does not work yet.
- **Each claim has an identity of its own, which the provisioner is given with every call**, and a proof, and whatever serves the hostname, belong to that claim alone: what an earlier claim left (its organization deleted, or its removal failed at the provisioner) never proves a later one, and removing a claim never touches a later one. The identities are ordered, from a sequence taken under the hostname's lock, never the clock, so an addition arriving late for an earlier claim is ignored, never replacing a later one. A domain keeps the claim it was proven by.
- **Removing revokes at once**: the domain's row goes, then the provisioner is told to forget that claim. Its failing is logged, not refused, since Braivo serves the hostname no more; what the provisioner still holds is its own to sweep. A domain the operator registered is not the provisioner's. The primary falls back to the next newest. The installation's own hostnames, those under its self-serve domain included, are not removed from the console, nor is an organization's last domain, so a working address remains.
- **Subdomains, by CNAME, first.** An apex domain (`fernwood.example`) works only where the organization's DNS provider flattens a CNAME at the apex (an ALIAS record), which the page says; Cloudflare's apex proxying, which gives fixed addresses, needs its Enterprise plan.
- **Refused as now** (white-label-2, -3): the installation's hostname, a hostname another organization has, and a malformed one; also any hostname under the installation's self-serve domain, which setup gives out by slug (access-31).

## Alternatives rejected

- **The core calling Cloudflare itself**, configured by environment. Self-hosters on Cloudflare would get it, but the open-source core would depend on one vendor's API and its plans.
- **The Domains page in Braivo Cloud only.** Every installation with its own provisioner would rebuild it, and the console would differ by where it runs.
- **A status column on `organization_domain`.** One table, but every security-sensitive read of it would need the filter, and one forgotten would trust an unproven hostname.
- **Holding the hostname while the provisioner removes it**, so no claim made meanwhile could be undone: a database transaction held open across a call to another service, and still no guard against a removal finishing after it gave up waiting. The claim's identity fences it instead.
- **Activating on a status read**, a `GET` with a side effect: simpler for the page, but reads would write, and preloading or a crawler could trigger it.
- **Proving ownership by DNS pointing at the installation alone.** A CNAME left behind by an organization that has gone would prove nothing about who claims it now.
- **A domain serving as soon as it is added**, as the operator's command does. Until DNS and the certificate are ready, the newest domain would be the primary one, and new links to learners would name a host that does not answer.
- **Apex domains by Cloudflare's apex proxying from the start.** It needs an Enterprise plan before any school has asked for an apex.

## Consequences

- ADR 0004's rule that `organization_domain` is never written on a member's word alone holds through the provisioner: members ask, the provisioner verifies, Braivo records what it reports; the operator still registers domains directly.
- Done in this repository: the list, `GET /api/organizations/:organizationId/domains`, and the console's Domains page (white-label-9); the provisioner port (`createServer`'s `domains`), the claim table and the hostname lock, and claiming by `POST`, from the console too, claims expiring after seven days (white-label-10); activating by `POST`, from the console too (white-label-11). Self-serve setup refuses `domains` and the other names an installation keeps for its own subdomains (access-31), so no organization holds the records' target.
- Still to do: the operator's cancelling a claim, and removing; on Braivo Cloud, the provisioner itself, `domains.braivo.app`, and Cloudflare for SaaS on its zones (outside this repository).
