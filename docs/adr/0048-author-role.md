# 0048: An `author` role prepares and publishes content, without administering the school

Status: accepted (2026-10-19). Amends [ADR 0039](0039-published-in-the-console.md) for who publishes, [ADR 0042](0042-groups-scope-teaching.md) for how organizations are listed, [ADR 0045](0045-remote-mcp.md) for which schools a connection may author in, and [ADR 0022](0022-machine-access.md), [ADR 0023](0023-mcp-server.md), [ADR 0028](0028-original-files.md), [ADR 0029](0029-server-drafting.md), and [ADR 0044](0044-course-numbers.md) where they named administrators for content.

## Context

A school's teachers prepare its material: they upload worksheets, turn them into practice, and decide what their learners see. Braivo let only an organization's `owner` or `admin` do that, because `member` is also every learner's role, and a learner must not author or record evidence. Teaching is a group role that grants reading the group's learners' progress and nothing else ([ADR 0042](0042-groups-scope-teaching.md)). So a teacher who should add a worksheet must be made `admin`, and then also manages members, groups, domains, and settings, and reads every learner's progress across the school, which groups were introduced to prevent.

## Decision

- **A new organization role, `author`,** beside `owner`, `admin`, and `member`, given and taken by an `owner` or `admin` (by the operator until the console can, Consequences). It grants managing all of the organization's learning content, other members' included: adding and deleting material ([ADR 0046](0046-material-in-steps.md)), drafting with Braivo's AI under the organization's existing gate and quota, without managing either ([ADR 0031](0031-ai-limits.md)), converting structured items ([ADR 0047](0047-structured-items.md)), objectives, citations, tasks, creating courses and arranging their objectives, publishing, retiring, hiding, and deleting what may be deleted. Which groups take a course stays administration's, since it decides whose progress a teacher reads ([ADR 0042](0042-groups-scope-teaching.md)).
- **Authors publish; channels keep their limits.** In a school the teacher is the reviewer, so publishing is authoring's last step, not administration's. An author acts in the console as an owner or admin does, and through tools ([ADR 0022](0022-machine-access.md), [ADR 0045](0045-remote-mcp.md)) only as any tool may: a tool still never publishes, retires a published task, changes a course's visibility, or deletes a course ([ADR 0039](0039-published-in-the-console.md)), whoever it acts for.
- **Authoring grants no access to people.** The `author` role grants no permission to list members, give or take roles, invite, define groups or their courses, read learners' progress or a course's overview of every member, record evidence, or change the organization's settings, domains, or name, through Braivo's routes or Better Auth's own. What an author reads of learners comes only from groups they teach (access-39), as for any member.
- **Roles are additive.** Any role is membership, so `author` alone lets someone study the school's courses as `member` does; `author` beside `admin` changes nothing. A teacher typically holds `author` and teaches some groups. Learners do not receive `author` unless an `owner` or `admin` grants it, to a teaching assistant, say. Taking `author` from someone who holds no other role leaves them a `member`, their learning history intact; removing them from the organization is a separate act. A role in one organization grants nothing in another, and an organization role never widens what a group lets someone read.
- **Content is the school's, not the author's.** Every author sees and changes every source, objective, and task of the organization, and every course's content (not which groups take it): a school's material is shared, a course draws on objectives others wrote, and correcting a colleague's task is ordinary review. Who wrote what is not recorded yet (authoring), a later decision once several authors share a school.
- **One check per operation, at every request.** Operations that create or manage content ask whether a member may author (`owner`, `admin`, or `author`); administration, reading learners' progress, and recording evidence for learners keep their own checks, wherever their routes or use cases live. A learner who is also an author still answers their own tasks as any learner does. Roles are read as each request runs, so a CLI session or MCP connection can start no authoring once its person loses the role; background work already accepted (an extraction, a draft, [ADR 0046](0046-material-in-steps.md)) runs or is cancelled as any job is, and its result is kept. The console's organization list keeps what it lists today, those someone administers or teaches in ([ADR 0042](0042-groups-scope-teaching.md)), adds those they author in, and says which apply, so the console shows an author Courses, Sources, the groups they teach, and their own settings; authoring tools find those they may author in by that answer (the CLI and remote MCP's consent offer only those; `braivo mcp` says which each is), while progress tools keep finding the groups someone teaches as before ([ADR 0042](0042-groups-scope-teaching.md), [ADR 0045](0045-remote-mcp.md)).

## Alternatives rejected

- **Making teachers `admin`**: every teacher manages people and settings and reads every class's progress.
- **Teaching a group grants authoring**: content is not a group's; a source or objective serves every course it is in, and a teacher of no group, a curriculum lead, could not author.
- **A role named `teacher`**: "teacher" is a group role already ([ADR 0042](0042-groups-scope-teaching.md)); one word for two things would blur who reads progress. The console may label `author` "Manage and publish content", saying it covers the whole organization's content.
- **Authors who only prepare, administrators who publish**: a review step the school's own teacher already is.
- **Content owned per author**: a school's teachers share and correct each other's material.

## Consequences

- The operator's `organization add-member` accepts `author`, enough for an assisted pilot; an administrator's way to give and take it in the console is needed before schools onboard themselves. Better Auth's access control learns the role as one with no organization permissions, so none of its routes lets an author promote themselves.
- Remote MCP's consent offers authoring for the schools one may author in, not only those one administers; a teacher of groups alone is offered none, and connects for their groups' progress once `braivo:progress` exists (ADR 0045).
- With several authors, recording who published or retired what, and later who wrote it, becomes worth deciding; until then an administrator cannot tell which colleague did.
