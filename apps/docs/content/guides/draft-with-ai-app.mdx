An AI app such as Claude or ChatGPT, once [connected to Braivo](./assistants.mdx#connect), can prepare a course from your material with its own model and your subscription to it. It acts as you, in one school, with your rights there. You need to be an Owner, Admin, or Author.

<div className="learning-loop">

1. Your AI app reads your material and writes questions
2. Braivo checks every quote against your material
3. The course waits, marked Needs review
4. You review and publish it
5. Learners practise it

</div>

## What to ask for

Ask for a small piece of work at a time, and say which part of your material, for whom, and what learners should be able to do.

| Ask like this                                                                                                      | Not like this                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| "From pages 12–18 of Spanish A1, prepare practice on definite articles for adult beginners who speak English."     | "Make me a Spanish course."    |
| "Turn chapter 2 of this PDF into a course for Grade 5: what plants take in and give out, one question per idea."   | "Make me a course on biology." |
| "Convert this question bank: keep every question's wording and answer key, and list the ones that need a picture." | "Rewrite these questions."     |

Bring a long book in as a file, in the console or with the [`braivo` command](./authoring.mdx#the-braivo-command), rather than through a chat: the app then reads it a part at a time. An app that retypes a book into its replies spends that many tokens, and can change words as it goes.

## What Braivo checks, and what it doesn't

Braivo checks:

- that every passage the app quotes is in your material, word for word, telling the app which one it couldn't find, so a paraphrase never passes for your words;
- that every question is valid, such as one right answer among its options.

Braivo doesn't check that an answer is right. A quote shows where a question came from, not that its answer key is correct, and a model retyping a statement can drop a "not" while quoting it correctly. That is yours to check when you review.

## Braivo's own draft, or your AI app

Braivo's own **Draft a course**, in the console, reads a whole source and takes only who the course is for. To draft from one chapter, add that chapter as a source of its own. Your AI app can also take pages, goals, and the kind of question you want.

## What it can and can't do

It can read and add material as text, define what the material teaches, write questions that quote it, correct them, and arrange them into a course. A hosted app adds the text it reads from what you attach; a desktop agent can also add a PDF with the file kept beside it, through the [`braivo` command](./authoring.mdx#the-braivo-command), which you let it run.

It can't publish anything, retire a published question, or read your learners' names or progress. Everything it writes reaches no learner until you publish it: [review and publish it](./review-and-publish.mdx) in the console.
